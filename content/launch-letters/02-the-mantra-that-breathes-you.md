The mantra that breathes you

There is a practice in the corpus called ajapa japa — the mantra that repeats itself. Not because a machine took over, but because it was never yours to begin with. Every breath you have taken since your first day outside the womb has said the same two syllables.

In: So. Out: Ham. I am. That am.

## The mechanics, honestly stated

This is not a mystical upgrade you install. It is a noticing you permit. The breath has been running the syllable the whole time; attention is the only thing that was absent. The old texts count this among mental japa — manasika — and rank it above spoken repetition for one plain reason: the spoken word ends when the voice ends. The breath does not end while you live.

## A small, safe beginning

- Sit once today — one minute is enough — and do not manufacture anything.
- Let the breath be exactly its ordinary size.
- Notice the turn at the top of the inhale and the bottom of the exhale. So'ham lives at the turns.
- When you forget, begin again. The beginning-again is the practice; nobody warns you about this and it is the truest thing in the whole literature.

No initiation is claimed here and none is needed to notice your own breath. What the tradition guards is depth, not the doorway.
