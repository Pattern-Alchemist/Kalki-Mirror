Why the archive stays silent

This letter is about a feature that looks like a defect. The Mirror refuses to answer some questions — and the refusal is load-bearing.

## What the archive will not do

The site has an ask surface: a question goes in, the corpus is searched, and an answer comes back with citations — the exact folios the answer was built from. What it will not do is improvise. When the corpus contains nothing that grounds an answer, the reply is a clean, honest silence: nothing found, nothing invented.

This was a design decision and it is tested in code. A mirror that answers everything is a horoscope. The value of the answer is exactly the value of the refusal that borders it.

## How the corpus is tiered

The practice material is held in two registers:

- OPEN — entry practices: breath, attention, rest, the patterns of mind. Published plainly, citable, yours to test.
- CAUTION — practices with real preconditions: force, risk, or the kind of depth that needs a living teacher. The corpus acknowledges these exist and does not hand them over in a paragraph.

This is not prudishness. Every honest lineage does the same thing with different furniture: it grades the road. The archive's silence on the second tier is the same silence a good doctor keeps about dosage.

## What the archive is for

The corpus answers where it can ground. It cites so you can check. It stays silent where it cannot, so that the line between knowledge and decoration stays visible. Five letters in, that is the whole editorial policy of this archive: nothing here asks to be believed — everything here asks to be tested.

If you have a question for it, the surface is here — and it will tell you plainly when it has nothing:

/ask

## The house rule, restated

- Where the corpus speaks, we cite.
- Where it is silent, we say so.
- Where the practice is graded, we grade it.

That is the entire constitution. The next letters follow the corpus wherever it goes — and keep the same promise.

— KALKI
