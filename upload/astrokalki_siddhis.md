## Dhāraṇā

### Trāṭaka

> त्राटक — Steady Gazing

*Haṭha / Rāja Yoga · Intermediate · authenticity: 92/100 · 30 days*

Fixed-gaze concentration, classically upon a candle flame, listed among the six purification acts (ṣaṭkarma) and as a gateway to inner vision.

**Primary mantra:** (silent fixation; optional internal Oṃ on the after-image)

**Warnings:**

- Practice candle trāṭaka with a stable flame on a fire-safe surface
- Limit to one session daily; over-practice can strain the eyes
- Discontinue if the eyes become painful rather than merely watery

**Evidence sources (1):**

- [high] *primary* — Gheraṇḍa Saṃhitā I.54; HYP II.32 ([source](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/hatha-yoga-pradipika)) — Lists trāṭaka among the purifications.

### Cakra Dhāraṇā

> चक्र धारणा — Concentration on the Centers

*Tantra · Intermediate · authenticity: 87/100 · 40 days*

Structured concentration upon the six principal psycho-spiritual centers (mūlādhāra → ājñā), each visualized with its element, color, and seed-syllable.

**Primary mantra:** laṃ · vaṃ · raṃ · yaṃ · haṃ · oṃ  (the ascending bījas)

**Warnings:**

- Intensive kuṇḍalinī work is held by tradition to require a qualified guide; symbolic visualization is the safer, open entry
- Discontinue if disturbing imagery or dissociation arises

**Evidence sources (1):**

- [high] *primary* — Ṣaṭ-Cakra-Nirūpaṇa vv. 1–55, 104–112 ([source](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shat-chakra-nirupana)) — Locations, elements, colors, bījas of the six cakras.

## Mantra

### Pranava Japa

> प्रणव जप — Oṃkāra Repetition

*Vedānta / Upaniṣadic · Foundation · authenticity: 97/100 · 21 days*

The contemplative repetition of Oṃ, the primal sound (pranava) held in the Upaniṣads to be the sonic signature of the Absolute.

**Primary mantra:** ॐ  (A — U — M — silence)

**Warnings:**

- Avoid strained vocalization that fatigues the throat
- Not a substitute for treatment of anxiety or depression

**Evidence sources (2):**

- [high] *primary* — Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad vv. 1–12 ([source](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/mandukya-upanishad)) — Parses Oṃ into A-U-M + silence (turīya).
- [high] *primary* — Yoga Sūtras I.27–28 ([source](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutras-of-patanjali)) — Designates Oṃ as the designator of īśvara; prescribes its contemplation.

### Gāyatrī Mantra

> गायत्री मन्त्र — Savitṛ Invocation

*Vedic (Ṛgveda) · Foundation · authenticity: 96/100 · 40 days*

A verse from the Ṛgveda (III.62.10) invoking the radiant principle (Savitṛ) to illuminate the intellect — among the most widely transmitted invocations in the tradition.

**Primary mantra:** tat savitur vareṇyaṃ bhargo devasya dhīmahi dhiyo yo naḥ pracodayāt

**Warnings:**

- Some lineages regard the mantra as requiring transmission (dīkṣā); study the range of views before practice
- Recitation is a contemplative act, not a guarantee of any worldly result

**Evidence sources (1):**

- [high] *primary* — Ṛgveda III.62.10 ([source](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/compilation/rig-veda)) — The original verse; oldest attestation.

### Maha Mṛtyuṅjaya Mantra

> महामृत्युंजय — The Great Death-Conquering Verse

*Vedic (Ṛgveda) · Intermediate · authenticity: 95/100 · 41 days*

A Ṛgvedic verse (VII.59.12) addressed to Tryambaka (the three-eyed one), petitioning liberation from mortality 'as a cucumber from its vine.'

**Primary mantra:** oṃ tryambakaṃ yajāmahe sugandhiṃ puṣṭivardhanam urvārukam iva bandhanān mṛtyor mukṣīya māmṛtāt

**Warnings:**

- Not a medical treatment; never framed as a substitute for care
- Some traditions treat it as best received through transmission

**Evidence sources (1):**

- [high] *primary* — Ṛgveda VII.59.12 ([source](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/compilation/rig-veda)) — The death-conquering verse.

### Mānasika Japa

> मानसिक जप — Mental Repetition

*Universal · Foundation · authenticity: 93/100 · 21 days*

The wholly internal repetition of a mantra, without movement of lips or breath — regarded as the most subtle and powerful mode of japa.

**Primary mantra:** (the practitioner's chosen seed or verse, repeated in silence)

**Warnings:**

- Drowsiness is the chief obstacle; keep the spine alert
- Do not regard mechanical counting as the goal — quality governs

**Evidence sources (1):**

- [medium] *primary* — Manu Smṛti II.85; Haṭha texts — Hierarchy of japa (vācika < upāṁśu < mānasika).

### Ajapa-Japa

> अजपाजप — The Spontaneous Breath-Mantra

*Nāth / Tantra · Intermediate · authenticity: 90/100 · 40 days*

The recognition that the breath itself sounds 'haṃ-saḥ' (So'haṃ) some 21,600 times a day — japa performed without effort of repetition.

**Primary mantra:** so'haṃ  ·  haṃsaḥ  (I am That)

**Warnings:**

- Do not impose the sound forcefully; the method is recognition, not production
- Remain seated and grounded; profound stillness can arise suddenly

**Evidence sources (1):**

- [high] *primary* — Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra vv. 24, 27 ([source](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/vijnana-bhairava-tantra)) — Breath-awareness dhāraṇās underlying ajapa.

## Meditation

### So'haṃ Dhyāna

> सोऽहं — 'I Am That' Non-dual Contemplation

*Vedānta / Nāth · Foundation · authenticity: 90/100 · 30 days*

Contemplative abidance in the recognition 'so'haṃ' — 'I am That' — the non-dual identity of the individual self (jīva) and the absolute (brahman).

**Primary mantra:** so'haṃ  ·  śivo'ham  ·  ahaṃ brahmāsmi

**Warnings:**

- Do not construe the recognition as a grandiose self-image
- Remain ordinary; the frame is humility disguised as simplicity

**Evidence sources (1):**

- [high] *primary* — Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad I.4.1 (sa ahaṃ asmī) — The seed-identity of jīva and brahman.

### Yoga Nidrā

> योग निद्रा — Conscious Sleep

*Tantra / Haṭha · Intermediate · authenticity: 89/100 · 28 days*

The state of 'conscious sleep' — the body resting while awareness remains luminous and unbroken, rooted in tantric and Upaniṣadic sources.

**Primary mantra:** I am awake, though the body sleeps.  (the witness-sentence)

**Warnings:**

- Practitioners with trauma or dissociative conditions should use gentle, shortened sessions
- Do not use while operating machinery or driving

**Evidence sources (2):**

- [high] *primary* — Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad vv. 5–7 ([source](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/mandukya-upanishad)) — Deep-sleep state (prājña) and the fourth (turīya).
- [medium] *modern* — Saraswati, S. — Yoga Nidrā (Bihar School, 1976) — Systematic modern protocol; distinguishes from the classical state.

## Prāṇāyāma

### Nāḍī Śuddhi

> नाडी शुद्धि — Channel Purification Breath

*Haṭha / Rāja Yoga · Foundation · authenticity: 94/100 · 14 days*

Alternate-nostril breathing (anuloma-viloma) used to balance the lunar (iḍā) and solar (piṅgalā) vital currents before deeper practice.

**Primary mantra:** (silence of measured breath — optional soft Oṃ on retention)

**Warnings:**

- Never force retention; stop if dizzy or strained
- Avoid during acute respiratory illness
- Pregnant practitioners should use gentle, unrestrained pacing

**Evidence sources (1):**

- [high] *primary* — Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā II.7–14 ([source](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/hatha-yoga-pradipika)) — Describes nāḍī-śuddhi prāṇāyāma.

### Kumbhaka

> कुम्भक — Breath Retention

*Haṭha / Rāja Yoga · Intermediate · authenticity: 91/100 · 30 days*

The deliberate, measured retention of breath (antar/sahita) described in the Yoga Sūtras as the means by which 'the veil over illumination is thinned.'

**Primary mantra:** (silent retention; the natural Oṃ of the held breath)

**Warnings:**

- Never retain to the point of gasping, dizziness, or distress
- Those with cardiovascular or respiratory conditions must proceed only with clinical guidance
- Build ratios gradually over weeks, not days

**Evidence sources (1):**

- [high] *primary* — Yoga Sūtras II.49–52 ([source](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutras-of-patanjali)) — Defines prāṇāyāma and retention; 'thins the veil.'

## Ritual

> सन्ध्यावन्दनम् — The Twilight Rites

*Vedic (Smārta) · Foundation · authenticity: 94/100 · 60 days*

The thrice-daily Vedic discipline performed at the junctures (sandhyā) of dawn, noon, and dusk — arguably the oldest continuously practiced contemplative ritual on earth.

**Primary mantra:** gāyatrī japa (108×) preceded by 'oṃ bhūr bhuvaḥ svaḥ...'

**Evidence sources (1):**

## Tantra

### Ṣoḍaśī / Tripurasundarī Sādhana

> षोडशी — The Sixteen-Year-Old Goddess

*Śrī Vidyā (Śākta) · Advanced · authenticity: 90/100 · 45 days*

The full pañcadaśākṣarī (15-syllable) sādhana of Tripurasundarī — the beautiful sixteen-year-old form of the Goddess, the central deity of Śrī Vidyā. Attested in the Vāmakeśvara Tantra, Bhāvanā Upaniṣad, Paraśurāma Kalpasūtra.

**Primary mantra:** the pañcadaśākṣarī (15-syllable) is dīkṣā-restricted in all published editions; the operational form is never written down and is given orally in dīkṣā

**Warnings:**

- HIGH CAUTION — advanced Śrī Vidyā practice; traditionally requires dīkṣā
- The pañcadaśākṣarī is held to require precise pronunciation and stable contemplative grounding; the source texts warn that incorrect japa is destabilising rather than merely ineffective
- The mūla-mantra is dīkṣā-restricted in all published editions — it is given orally and never written down
- The Bālā Tripurasundarī sādhana is traditionally the prerequisite; without that foundation, Ṣoḍaśī practice is held to be premature
- Not appropriate for practitioners without a Śrī Vidyā lineage holder

**Evidence sources (9):**

- [high] *primary* — Vāmakeśvara Tantra / Nityāṣoḍaśikārṇava (Anandāśrama 56, Internet Archive) ([source](https://archive.org/details/vamakeshvara-tantra-anandashrama-56)) — Primary Śrī Vidyā text attesting the pañcadaśākṣarī.
- [high] *primary* — Vāmakeśvara Tantra (Sampurnanand 1985 ed., Internet Archive) ([source](https://archive.org/details/vamakeshvara-tantra-sampurnanand-1985)) — Alternate digitised edition.
- [high] *primary* — Vāmakeśvarī Mātam with Jayaratha's Vivaraṇa (trans. Sampath Sharma) ([source](https://archive.org/details/vamakeshvari-matam-jayaratha)) — Edition with Jayaratha's commentary translated.
- [high] *primary* — Bhāvanā Upaniṣad (Śrī Cakra and pañcadaśī) — Upaniṣadic description of the Śrī Cakra and the 15-syllable mantra.
- [high] *primary* — Paraśurāma Kalpa Sūtra (4 Internet Archive editions, c. 10th-11th c.) ([source](https://archive.org/details/parashurama-kalpa-sutra)) — Primary Śrī Vidyā paddhati with commentaries.
- [high] *primary* — Lalitā Sahasranāma with Bhāskararāya's Saubhāgya-Bhāskara (Adyar 1925) ([source](https://archive.org/details/lalita-sahasranama-saubhagya-bhaskara)) — Standard commentary on the Lalitā Sahasranāma.

### Dakṣiṇa Kālī Sādhana

> दक्षिणकाली — The Benevolent Fierce Form

*Śākta (Daśa Mahāvidyā — first) · Advanced · authenticity: 88/100 · 45 days*

The primary Mahāvidyā form of Kālī — the first of the ten Mahāvidyās, iconographically depicted standing on Śiva, four-armed, holding a cleaver and a severed head, wearing a garland of severed heads and a skirt of severed arms. Attested in the Karpūrādi Stotra, Kālī Tantra, Todala Tantra.

**Primary mantra:** the mūla-mantra is dīkṣā-restricted in published editions; the publicly attested bīja-frame is 'oṃ krīṁ hrīṁ hūṃ dakṣiṇa kālike namaḥ'

**Warnings:**

- HIGH CAUTION — advanced Mahāvidyā practice; traditionally requires dīkṣā
- The Kālī Tantra prescribes specific nyāsa, āvāhana, and protective preliminaries; without these the japa is held to be ineffective or destabilising
- The mūla-mantra is dīkṣā-restricted in all published editions
- Not appropriate for practitioners without a Śākta-Kālī lineage grounding

**Evidence sources (6):**

- [high] *primary* — Todala Tantra (ch. 6, mūla-mantras of the 10 Mahāvidyās) ([source](https://archive.org/details/todala-tantra)) — Lists Kālī as the first Mahāvidyā.

### Lakulīśa Pāśupata Sādhana

> लाकुलीश पाशुपत — The Pāśupata Ascetic Path

*Śaiva (Pāśupata) · Advanced · authenticity: 86/100*

The Pāśupata Śaiva ascetic path attributed to Lakulīśa (2nd c. CE) — the elder Śaiva tradition that influenced later tantra. Attested in the Pāśupata Sūtras with Kauṇḍinya's Pañcārthabhāṣya (Trivandrum Sanskrit Series 143, 1940).

**Primary mantra:** the Pāśupata path is primarily a vows-and-conduct framework rather than a mantra-centric sādhana; the Pāśupata Sūtras do not document a mūla-mantra in the tantric sense. The Namaskāra to Śiva (Namaḥ Śivāya) is the public pañcākṣara used in the Pāśupata preliminaries.

**Warnings:**

- HIGH CAUTION — extreme ascetic practice; traditionally requires formal initiation into a Pāśupata order
- The stage-bound conduct (ācāra) includes paradoxical behaviours (acting as if mad, deliberately attracting contempt) that are destabilising if undertaken without supervision
- The Pāśupata path is not mantra-centric in the tantric sense; do not impose tantric frameworks on it
- Not appropriate for practitioners without a Pāśupata lineage holder; this is a scholarly entry-point, not a practice manual

**Evidence sources (5):**

- [high] *primary* — Pāśupata Sūtras with Kauṇḍinya's Pañcārthabhāṣya, Trivandrum Sanskrit Series 143 (1940, ed. Ananta Kṛṣṇa Śāstrī) ([source](https://archive.org/details/pashupata-sutras-tss-143)) — Primary text + only surviving ancient commentary. 3 Internet Archive digitisations incl. English translation.
- [high] *primary* — Pashupat Sutram (Chakrapani Trivedi ed., Shaiva Bharati Varanasi #54) ([source](https://archive.org/details/pashupat-sutram-trivedi)) — Alternate edition with Hindi commentary.
- [high] *scholarly* — Daniel Ingalls, 'Cynics and Pāśupatas' (HJAS, 1962) — Foundational academic article on the Pāśupata tradition.
- [high] *scholarly* — Alexis Sanderson, 'Śaivism and the Tantric Traditions' (1985); 'The Śaiva Exegesis of Kashmir' (2009) — Scholarly survey of the Śaiva milieu within which the Pāśupata tradition is situated.
- [medium] *scholarly* — Peter Bisschop, Early Śaivism and the Skandapurāṇa (2006) — Scholarly analysis of the early Śaiva traditions including Pāśupata.

### Śānti Karma (Pacification)

> शान्तिकर्म — The Pacifying Act

*Śākta / Ṣaṭ-karma · Intermediate · authenticity: 85/100 · 41 days*

The first of the six ṣaṭ-karma — pacification. The calming of hostile forces, the settling of disturbances, the restoration of equilibrium. Attested in the Agni Purāṇa ch. 138, Prapañcasāra Tantra, Śāradā Tilaka.

**Primary mantra:** the mūla-mantra varies by lineage; the publicly attested form is 'oṃ śāntiṁ kuru kuru svāhā'

**Warnings:**

- MODERATE CAUTION — the gentlest ṣaṭ-karma, but still requires dīkṣā for the mūla-mantra
- The Agni Purāṇa prescribes specific kalāśa material (clay) and color (white); without these the rite is held to be ineffective
- Not appropriate for practitioners without a Śākta lineage grounding

- The mūla-mantra is dīkṣā-restricted in published editions
- Not appropriate for practitioners without a Śrī Vidyā lineage holder

**Evidence sources (5):**
